01 The Snap Event — Anomaly Mechanics

The "Thanos Snap" hits at Jan 1 19:00. Every sector experiences an instant ~50% stock drop in a single hour. But critically, some sectors show partial recovery the very next hour. This isn't just a crash — it's a crash-and-scramble pattern. The data has 5 readings per hour per sector, and during the snap the readings diverge wildly (some sensors catch the drop, one may not). This is rich.

Insight: Snap Detection Algorithm

stock_level timestamp hour-over-hour % change cross-sector correlation

The data: At 18:00, Wakanda averages ~704 stock. At 19:00, the 5 readings split: one at 704 (normal), four between 334–377 (snapped). By 20:00 some readings bounce to 693 while others stay around 350. The snap isn't uniform — it's a probabilistic event where ~80% of sensors detect it.

The computation: For each hour, compute the average stock across the 5 readings. If the hour-over-hour change exceeds −30% AND this is correlated across 3+ sectors simultaneously, flag it as a "snap-class anomaly." Simple Z-score on the hourly delta works: anything beyond −2σ from the rolling mean delta gets flagged.

snap_score = |Δstock_avg(t)| / rolling_std(Δstock_avg, window=24h)
IF snap_score > 2.0 AND sectors_affected >= 3 → SNAP ALERT

Display: A timeline chart with a red vertical annotation line at the snap. Show a "before/after" comparison card: stock levels 1 hour pre-snap vs. post-snap for all 5 sectors. This becomes the centerpiece when judges ask "how did you handle the snap?"

annotated time-series chart before/after comparison card snap alert banner

Insight: Sensor Divergence as an Early Warning

stock_level (5 per hour) intra-hour standard deviation intra-hour spread (max − min)

The data: At hour 0 all 5 readings are identical (800.0). By hour 4, the spread is already 19.9. By hour 10 the 5 sensors are diverging by 28+. During the snap itself, a single hour has one reading at 704 and another at 334 — a spread of 370.

The computation: Compute std_dev and spread of the 5 readings each hour. A rising spread is a precursor signal that something unstable is happening. Normal divergence follows a pattern; snap divergence is an order of magnitude higher.

sensor_confidence = 1 - (std_dev(readings_t) / mean(readings_t))
IF sensor_confidence < 0.7 → "SENSOR INSTABILITY — verify readings"

Display: A "data confidence" indicator next to each stock reading. Green = all 5 sensors agree. Yellow = moderate spread. Red = sensors are wildly disagreeing. This is a subtle but powerful indicator that something is about to go wrong.

02 Depletion Forecasting — Going Beyond Linear

The naive approach is linear regression on stock_level over time. But the data is far more interesting: there are resupply events (stock jumps back up), the snap causes a discontinuity, and usage rates vary between 2–8/hr. Here are the specific forecasting models you could implement and what they'd show.

Model A: Net Burn Rate with Resupply Detection

stock_level (hourly avg) usage_rate_hourly net burn = consumption − resupply time-to-zero

The insight: Resupply events are frequent (56–134 per sector over 17 days). Wakanda gets ~56 resupply bumps, but each resupply gets smaller over time: early resupplies add ~260 units, late ones add only ~50–60. The supply chain is degrading.

The computation: Separate consumption (stock goes down) from resupply (stock goes up) events. Compute avg consumption/hr and avg resupply/hr. The net burn rate = consumption − resupply. When net burn is positive, the sector is dying. When it flips to negative, they've stabilized.

net_burn_rate = avg(consumption_events/hr) − avg(resupply_events/hr)
time_to_zero = current_stock / net_burn_rate

Wakanda: consumption ≈ 5/hr, resupply_freq declining → net_burn accelerating

Display: Two lines on the chart — one for "consumption only" projection and one for "with resupply" projection. The gap between them narrows over time as resupply fails. Show the exact hour where they converge (point of no return).

dual-line forecast chart "point of no return" marker resupply health indicator

Model B: Monte Carlo Probability Cones

usage_rate_hourly (std_dev ≈ 1.73) resupply_magnitude (declining) P(depletion) at each future hour

The insight: Usage rates have a standard deviation of ~1.73 across all sectors. That means any hour's consumption could be anywhere from ~1 to ~9 units. A single linear prediction hides this uncertainty.

The computation: Run 500–1000 simulations. Each simulation samples usage rates from a normal distribution with the sector's observed mean and std. Optionally include resupply as a probabilistic event (e.g., 15% chance per hour, magnitude drawn from historical resupply sizes). For each simulation, record when stock hits zero. Aggregate into percentile bands.

for sim in 1..1000:
  for hour in future_hours:
    usage = normal(mean=4.7, std=1.73)
    resupply = bernoulli(p=0.15) * normal(mean=100, std=50)
    stock -= usage; stock += resupply
    if stock <= 0: record(depletion_hour); break

Result: P(depletion) = count(depleted) / 1000 at each hour

Display: A fan/cone chart. The center line is the median projection. The shaded bands show 50th percentile (dark) and 90th percentile (light). This gives commanders a range: "best case 3 days, worst case 18 hours, most likely 2 days."

probability cone chart depletion probability ticker

Model C: Snap-Aware Regime Detection

snap_event_detected stock_level (pre/post snap) regime classification separate forecast per regime

The insight: Pre-snap, sectors have 800–2500 starting stock and steady usage. Post-snap, the same sectors have ~half the stock but the same usage rate. Usage does NOT drop to match the lower supply. This means the snap cuts supply but not demand — a recipe for accelerated collapse.

The computation: Split the time series at the snap event. Train separate models for "normal regime" and "crisis regime." For forecasting, use the crisis-regime model since we're post-snap. Optionally, detect if a new snap is occurring and switch models in real-time.

Pre-snap avg usage: 4.73/hr, Post-snap avg usage: 5.16/hr (+9%)
→ Usage actually INCREASES post-snap (panic consumption?)
→ Crisis-regime model must account for demand surge

Display: The time-series chart with a clear visual divider between regimes. Different colored forecast lines for each regime. A "current regime" indicator badge on the dashboard.

03 Resupply Chain Intelligence

This is a finding most teams will miss. The stock doesn't just go down — it repeatedly bounces back up. These resupply events happen dozens of times per sector. But the resupply magnitude is declining over time. The supply chain is dying.

Insight: Supply Chain Health Score

stock_level jumps (Δ > +50) resupply frequency (events/day) resupply magnitude (avg units added) supply chain health index

The data: Wakanda has 56 resupply events over 17 days. Early resupplies (Jan 1-3) average +150 units. Late resupplies (Jan 10+) average +60 units. The frequency also drops: from ~5/day to ~2/day. The supply chain is collapsing at both frequency AND magnitude.

supply_health = (resupply_freq_recent / resupply_freq_baseline) × (resupply_mag_recent / resupply_mag_baseline)

Wakanda: (2/5) × (60/150) = 0.16 → supply chain at 16% health

Display: A gauge/donut chart per sector showing "Supply Chain Health %" declining over time. Color-coded: green > 70%, yellow 30–70%, red < 30%. This is a leading indicator — it shows a sector is in trouble BEFORE stock hits zero.

supply chain health gauge resupply trend sparkline days-until-supply-chain-failure

Insight: "Secured Cache" Reports = Resupply Signals

field_intel_reports.json historical_avengers_data.csv cross-validation score

The connection: 39 of 200 field reports are "Status update from {SECTOR}. We secured a cache of {RESOURCE}." These are the human-reported resupply events. If the LLM extracts "sector=Wakanda, resource=Clean Water, event_type=RESUPPLY" from these reports, you can cross-reference with the CSV to see if a corresponding stock increase actually appears in the time series.

The catch: 80% of "secured cache" reports mention a resource that the sector doesn't actually track in the CSV (e.g., Wakanda securing Clean Water when Wakanda only tracks Arc Reactor Cores). This is either noise or it implies there's a broader resource network the CSV doesn't capture.

Display: In the intel feed, when a "secured cache" report comes in, auto-link it to the relevant time series chart with a green marker. If the claim is verified by the data, show a ✓. If the sector doesn't track that resource, show a ⚠ with "Unverified — resource not tracked for this sector."

04 Field Intel — Structured Entity Extraction

The 200 reports follow exactly 5 templates. The LLM's job is to extract structured fields from each one. Here's what each template yields and what you can compute from the extracted data.

Template (47–32 each) Extractable Fields Implied Severity Action Type
"critically low on {RESOURCE}" sector, resource, severity=critical CRITICAL — nearing depletion Emergency resupply
"Heavy combat… supply chain compromised" sector, resource, cause=combat, status=supply_chain_disrupted HIGH — active threat to supply Secure supply line + combat support
"secured a cache of {RESOURCE}" sector, resource, event_type=resupply POSITIVE — incoming supply Update stock forecast upward
"situation is dire… we need more" sector, resource, severity=dire, risk=perimeter_loss CRITICAL — operational risk Priority resupply + reinforcement
"out of {RESOURCE}" sector, resource, severity=depleted, reporter_name (PII!), phone (PII!) DEPLETED — stock is zero Immediate intervention

Derived Metric: Sector Threat Score (combining both datasets)

CSV: stock_level, usage_rate JSON: report severity, report count composite threat score 0–100

The formula: Weight multiple signals together into a single per-sector score:

threat_score = w1 × (1 − stock_pct) + w2 × depletion_velocity + w3 × critical_report_density + w4 × supply_chain_decay

where:
  stock_pct = current_stock / max_stock
  depletion_velocity = avg_usage / current_stock (hours to zero)
  critical_report_density = critical_reports_last_6h / total_reports_last_6h
  supply_chain_decay = 1 − supply_health_score

suggested weights: w1=0.3, w2=0.3, w3=0.2, w4=0.2

This is the integration moment. The threat score uses CSV data (stock levels, usage rates) AND JSON data (report severity, frequency). It's the single metric that proves both datasets are connected in your system. A sector can have moderate stock levels but a HIGH threat score if field reports are screaming about combat and supply disruption.

Display: Large, color-coded threat score card per sector on the main dashboard. Think "DEFCON level" styling: green (0–25), yellow (25–50), orange (50–75), red (75–100). Each card expandable to show which factors are driving the score.

threat score card per sector factor breakdown drill-down threat trend sparkline

Derived Metric: Intel Reliability Score

JSON: sector + resource claim CSV: actual stock for that sector claim vs reality accuracy

The insight: 80% of reports mention a sector-resource pair that doesn't match the CSV's actual mapping (e.g., a report about Wakanda's Clean Water when Wakanda only tracks Arc Reactor Cores). Only 40 of 200 reports have a "correct" sector-resource match.

Interpretation options:

1. Field confusion: Operatives on the ground don't know which sector tracks which resource — they're reporting what they observe. The system should still log the claim but flag it as "unverifiable against telemetry."

2. Hidden supply needs: Wakanda may need Clean Water even though the CSV only tracks Arc Reactor Cores there. The reports reveal unmonitored resource gaps. Your system should surface these as "blind spots" in sensor coverage.

3. Disinformation filtering: If the data conflicts, maybe the report is wrong. A reliability scoring system could weight reports higher when they match known telemetry and lower when they don't.

reliability_score(report) = {
  1.0 if sector-resource matches CSV AND stock_level confirms claim,
  0.7 if sector matches but resource not tracked (plausible but unverified),
  0.3 if claim contradicts CSV data (e.g., "critically low" but stock is high)
}

05 PII Detection — Specific Patterns in This Data

PII leaks are concentrated in exactly ONE template: the "out of" reports. All 34 PII-leaking reports follow the pattern: "Just a heads up, {SECTOR} is out of {RESOURCE}. This is {NAME}, call me back at {PHONE}." The name and phone always appear together. Here's exactly what to detect.

Name Patterns to Catch

Tony Stark
Natasha Romanoff
Thor Odinson
Peter Parker
Bruce Banner
Steve Rogers

All appear as "This is {FULL_NAME}, call me back" — dictionary match on known names works. But for demo purposes, also add NER (spaCy/compromise.js) to show you'd catch unknown names too.

Phone Patterns to Catch

555-0101 (Iron Line)
555-0199 (Black Widow Comms)
555-GOD-OF-THUNDER
555-0123 (Spider-Sense)
555-HULK-SMASH
555-0100 (Captain Comms)
555-1941 (Shield Freq)

Mix of numeric (555-0101) and alphanumeric (555-GOD-OF-THUNDER). A single regex won't catch both. Need: regex for 555-\d{4} + regex for 555-[A-Z-]+ + catch the parenthetical suffix.

catch unknown PERSON entities

Key demo detail: 166 of 200 reports have NO PII in the raw_text (it's only in metadata). Make sure your demo shows one of the 34 "out of" reports — those are the ones with visible before/after redaction. If you accidentally demo a "critically low" report, there's nothing to redact and the security demo falls flat.

06 The Integration Layer — Where Both Datasets Meet

This is the highest-value section. These are the specific moments where the CSV telemetry data and the JSON field reports reinforce, contradict, or extend each other.

Relationship 1: Report Volume Correlates with Stock Depletion

CSV: stock levels approaching zero JSON: report count per sector reports-per-hour vs stock-pct

Reports are timestamped Jan 15–16, exactly when Wakanda (0 stock), Sokovia (0 stock), and Avengers Compound (0 stock) are fully depleted. Sanctum Sanctorum and New Asgard still have some stock. A model could show: as stock_pct drops below 20%, field report frequency spikes. This is the "human signal confirms the sensor signal" moment.

Display: Overlay report count as bar chart on top of the stock time-series line chart. Show the correlation visually: reports cluster where stock is lowest.

dual-axis chart: stock line + report bar overlay

Relationship 2: Report Severity vs Actual Stock Level

JSON: priority field (Routine/High/Avengers Level Threat) CSV: stock_level at report timestamp severity-weighted stock alert

The priority field in reports (Routine: 76, High: 63, Avengers Level: 61) is randomly assigned — it does NOT correlate with actual stock levels. A "Routine" report can say "critically low" while an "Avengers Level Threat" can be a cache secured. This means the priority field is unreliable.

The opportunity: Your LLM-derived severity (from text analysis) is MORE accurate than the metadata priority. Show this! Display a comparison: "Report metadata says Routine. LLM text analysis says CRITICAL. CSV data confirms stock is at 0. → System overrides priority to CRITICAL." This is a powerful moment.

priority override badge "AI-corrected severity" column

Relationship 3: Unmonitored Resource Alerts (Blind Spots)

JSON: sector-resource pairs mentioned CSV: sector-resource pairs tracked unmonitored resource flags

The CSV only tracks ONE resource per sector. But field reports mention ALL 5 resources across ALL 5 sectors. 160 of 200 reports reference a sector-resource pair that has no telemetry data. These are intelligence about problems the sensor network can't see.

Display: A 5×5 matrix (sectors × resources). The diagonal (tracked pairs) is filled with live data. Off-diagonal cells are grayed out but populated with "HUMINT only" flags from field reports. When a field report fills in an unmonitored cell, it lights up with a ⚠ icon. This instantly shows commanders where their sensor coverage has gaps.

Relationship 4: Depletion Confirmation Chain

CSV: stock hits zero at specific timestamp JSON: "out of" reports near that timestamp time-to-confirmation (telemetry → HUMINT)

When the CSV shows a sector's stock hitting 0, do field reports confirm it? Wakanda hit zero on Jan 5 in the CSV. Reports from Jan 15 say "Wakanda is out of Arc Reactor Cores." That's a 10-day lag between sensor detection and human report. For Sokovia (zero on Jan 5), reports on Jan 15–16 confirm "Sokovia is out of Pym Particles."

07 Usage Rate Intelligence

Insight: Usage INCREASES When Stock Hits Zero (Phantom Demand)

usage_rate_hourly stock_level usage at zero stock vs high stock

The finding: When stock is high (>500), average usage is ~4.3/hr. When stock hits ZERO, usage jumps to ~5.5/hr. That's a 28% increase in demand when there's nothing left. For Sanctum Sanctorum and New Asgard, the jump is even more dramatic: 4.7/hr at high stock → 7.4/hr at zero stock (57% increase).

Interpretation: This models panic consumption or black-market demand. When people know something is running out, they hoard. Or it could represent unmet demand that's being logged even though fulfillment is impossible. Either way, it's a critical forecasting input: once stock drops below a threshold, assume demand will SPIKE, not stay constant.

IF stock_pct < 10%:
  adjusted_usage = base_usage × 1.28 (low-stock sectors)
  adjusted_usage = base_usage × 1.57 (high-capacity sectors)

This tightens your time-to-zero estimate significantly

Insight: Two Tiers of Sectors

starting stock_level time-to-first-zero sector resilience tier

The data clearly shows two groups:

Tier 1 — Fragile (start at 800 stock): Wakanda, Sokovia, Avengers Compound. Hit zero by Jan 5 (~4.5 days). Low initial reserves. Completely depleted well before the reports come in.

Tier 2 — Resilient (start at 2500 stock): Sanctum Sanctorum, New Asgard. Don't hit zero until Jan 14 (~13 days). 3× the initial reserves. Still partially operational during the reporting period.

Display: Tier classification badges on each sector. A "sector resilience ranking" that orders sectors by remaining operational time. This helps commanders prioritize: Tier 1 sectors need immediate intervention, Tier 2 sectors have a window.
